

# Engineering the Marine Sulfur Cycle



## From DMSP metabolism to a controllable biological system

The ocean is not only a vast ecosystem.

It is also a complex biochemical network, where microorganisms transform molecules that connect marine ecosystems, atmospheric chemistry, and the global environment.

Among these molecules, **dimethylsulfoniopropionate (DMSP)** plays an important role in the marine sulfur cycle.

---

## The Challenge

DMSP is one of the most abundant organic sulfur compounds in the ocean.

However, its biological transformation is highly complex. Different enzymes and microorganisms can direct DMSP toward different metabolic products, including **dimethyl sulfide (DMS)**.

The efficiency and controllability of these transformations remain important challenges for synthetic biology and metabolic engineering.

> **How can we transform a naturally occurring metabolic pathway into a more efficient and controllable biological system?**

---

## Our Vision

We aim to develop a controllable biological system for DMSP conversion.

Our project focuses on three central questions:

- How can we improve the efficiency of key enzymes?
- How can we organize multiple enzymes into an effective metabolic pathway?
- How can we introduce regulatory mechanisms to make the system more controllable?

Rather than optimizing a single enzyme in isolation, we seek to engineer the entire biological system.

---

## Our Engineering Strategy

Our project is built around a modular strategy.

### 1. Improve enzyme performance

We focus on improving the conversion efficiency of key enzymes involved in DMSP metabolism.

Through protein engineering and rational design, we aim to improve the performance of the pathway's key catalytic steps.

---

### 2. Build the core metabolic module

We first establish a simplified and experimentally verifiable metabolic module.

This allows us to independently evaluate each component before integrating the complete pathway.

---

### 3. Integrate downstream metabolism

After validating the core module, we introduce downstream enzymatic reactions to expand the metabolic pathway.

This staged strategy allows us to gradually construct a more complete sulfur metabolic system while reducing experimental complexity.

---

### 4. Organize enzymes in space

Metabolic efficiency is not determined only by enzyme activity.

The spatial organization of enzymes can also influence the concentration of intermediates and the efficiency of substrate transfer.

Therefore, we explore the use of **scaffold-based enzyme co-localization** to bring pathway enzymes into closer proximity.

---

### 5. Create a controllable system

A biological system should not only function.

It should also be controllable.

We therefore explore regulatory strategies that can connect environmental or cellular signals with pathway activity.

Our long-term goal is to develop a system that can dynamically regulate sulfur metabolism according to biological conditions.
